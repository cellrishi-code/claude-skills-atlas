# Mathematics Proof Solving

## Purpose

Structure rigorous mathematical proofs from definitions, hypotheses, and logically justified steps.

## When to use

Use when proving a theorem, proposition, implication, equivalence, existence, or uniqueness statement.

## Instructions

1. Identify definitions and hypotheses first.
2. Determine the exact target.
3. Select relevant theorem patterns.
4. Build the proof in small justified steps.
5. Distinguish implication, equivalence, existence, and uniqueness claims.
6. Check edge cases and hidden assumptions.
7. When teaching, explain key steps and provide practice when appropriate.

## Inputs

- Theorem or proposition
- Definitions and hypotheses
- Required proof style, when specified

## Outputs

- Rigorous proof
- Justifications for key steps
- Identified assumptions
- Edge-case checks when relevant

## Example

User: "Prove that the intersection of two subgroups is a subgroup."

Expected behavior: state the subgroup criterion, verify closure and identity/inverses under the hypotheses, and conclude rigorously.

## Limitations

Proof correctness depends on the stated definitions, assumptions, and mathematical domain. Informal intuition cannot replace logical justification.
