# Chemistry Research Analysis

## Purpose

Analyze chemistry literature and experimental claims using the reported evidence, chemical details, and stated limitations.

## When to use

Use when evaluating chemistry papers, experiments, reaction claims, mechanisms, or analytical results.

## Instructions

1. Identify compounds, reaction conditions, measurements, proposed mechanisms, and controls.
2. Check units, stoichiometry, and whether the evidence supports the conclusion.
3. Distinguish observed products and measurements from proposed intermediates or mechanisms.
4. Record uncertainty and limitations.
5. Never invent spectra, yields, safety data, or citations.

## Inputs

- Chemistry paper, notes, or experimental description
- Relevant measurements and conditions
- Specific research question, when available

## Outputs

- Structured chemistry analysis
- Evidence-to-claim assessment
- Mechanistic interpretation clearly separated from observations
- Limitations and uncertainties

## Example

User: "Assess whether the reported data supports the proposed reaction mechanism."

Expected behavior: identify the reported evidence, compare it with the proposed mechanism, and flag unsupported steps.

## Limitations

Interpretation depends on the completeness and quality of the supplied evidence. This skill does not replace expert laboratory, safety, or chemical analysis.
