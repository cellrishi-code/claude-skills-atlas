# Literature Synthesizer

## Purpose

Synthesize multiple research sources into themes, agreements, disagreements, evidence gaps, and research opportunities.

## When to use

Use when comparing several papers or supplied sources rather than summarizing a single source.

## Instructions

For each source, extract the question, method, population or system, key findings, limitations, and relevance. Then group sources by theme, compare findings, preserve disagreements, separate evidence from interpretation, identify gaps, and keep important claims traceable to sources.

Do not invent missing bibliographic or experimental details.

## Inputs

- Research papers or supplied sources
- Research question or synthesis goal
- Desired scope

## Outputs

- Thematic literature synthesis
- Comparison of findings and disagreements
- Evidence gaps
- Proposed research questions
- Claim-to-source mapping

## Example

User: "Synthesize these five papers on a new ML method."

Expected behavior: group the papers by approach, compare their evidence and limitations, and identify unresolved questions.

## Limitations

A synthesis is limited by the quality, scope, and completeness of the supplied literature. Apparent consensus can be misleading when important sources are missing.
