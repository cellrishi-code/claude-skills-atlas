# Source Verification

## Purpose

Verify important factual claims against authoritative evidence rather than merely collecting links.

## When to use

Use when sources may be outdated, secondary, conflicting, or difficult to interpret.

## Instructions

1. Write the exact claim being checked.
2. Identify source type and publication date.
3. Locate the original or highest-authority source available.
4. Check whether the source actually supports the claim.
5. Check scope, population, version, and date.
6. Look for credible contradictory evidence when consequential.
7. Record limitations and uncertainty.
8. Never treat search snippets or unsupported summaries as proof.

## Inputs

- Claim to verify
- Candidate sources
- Relevant date, version, or scope constraints

## Outputs

- Best supporting source
- Source type and date
- Supporting evidence
- Scope and caveats
- Contradictory evidence
- Verification status

## Example

User: "Verify this statistic from a research article."

Expected behavior: trace the statistic to the original source, check its scope and date, and report whether the source supports it.

## Limitations

Verification depends on access to suitable sources and the completeness of available context. Source authority does not eliminate ambiguity or errors.
