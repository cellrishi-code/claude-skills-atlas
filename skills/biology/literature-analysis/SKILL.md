# Biology Literature Analysis

## Purpose

Analyze biology research papers systematically using experimental design, controls, endpoints, statistical evidence, and limitations.

## When to use

Use when reviewing biology literature or comparing biological research claims.

## Instructions

1. Identify the biological system and research question.
2. Extract experimental design, controls, endpoints, and statistical evidence.
3. Distinguish correlation from causal evidence.
4. Distinguish model-system findings from broader claims.
5. Separate reported findings from interpretation.
6. Flag missing information rather than guessing.
7. Never invent biological results or citations.

## Inputs

- Biology paper or supplied text
- Research question
- Figures, methods, or results when available

## Outputs

- Structured literature analysis
- Evidence and methodology assessment
- Causal and model-system caveats
- Limitations and open questions

## Example

User: "Does this study establish that gene X causes the observed phenotype?"

Expected behavior: examine controls, experimental design, and evidence for causality before answering.

## Limitations

Literature analysis cannot replace domain expertise or reproduce experiments. Conclusions are limited by the supplied paper and evidence quality.
